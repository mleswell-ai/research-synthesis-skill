# Does peer mentoring improve retention of new nurses? A systematic review (fictitious draft)

## Introduction

Retention of newly qualified nurses is a well-known problem. Research shows that peer
mentoring is highly effective.

## Review of the literature

Adeyemi (2019) studied 60 nurses in one hospital and found that mentored nurses were more
satisfied. Brandt and Liu (2020) found that mentoring improved retention. Costa (2021)
found a significant effect on retention. Duarte et al. (2018) found no significant effect
in a trial of 48 nurses. Egan (2022) found a significant effect. A press article about
Costa (2021) also reported that mentoring improves retention. Farrow (2017) found no
significant effect.

Overall, five out of seven sources found that mentoring works, so the evidence is strong.
Costa (2021) reported that mentoring cut first-year attrition by 62%, proving that peer
mentoring causes nurses to stay. Experts agree that all hospitals should adopt it. It is
well established that mentoring also improves patient outcomes (Hallam, 2020).

## Conclusion

Peer mentoring is proven to improve retention and should be mandatory nationally.
