# Flexible start times in distribution: an 18-month matched evaluation (fictitious)

Institute for Work Research, independent evaluation funded by a research council, 2024.

## Method (pages 4 to 6)

We compared 9 depots that introduced flexible start times with 9 matched depots at other
employers that did not, matching on region, size, pay band and prior turnover. 2,046
staff. Depots were not randomly assigned. Outcomes were taken from payroll records.

## Findings (pages 12 to 15)

Table 5. Voluntary turnover

| Period | Flexible depots | Matched depots | Difference (95% CI) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Months 1 to 6 | 6.1% | 8.4% | -2.3 points (-4.6 to 0.0) |
| Months 7 to 18 | 14.9% | 14.2% | +0.7 points (-2.4 to 3.8) |

Turnover was lower in flexible depots in the first six months, but there was no
difference over the following year. In interviews, managers at five of the nine flexible
depots said that popular start slots filled quickly and that newer staff were usually
left with the least popular ones (page 17).
