# Failure modes

Each entry gives the symptom, the cause and the fix. Use it to check a draft, to audit
someone else's synthesis, or when something feels wrong.

## Contents

- Failures of synthesis
- Failures of evidence handling
- Failures of calibration
- Failures of process
- Failures of reporting

## Failures of synthesis

**Serial summary.** *Symptom*: paragraphs that begin "Source A found... Source B
found...". *Cause*: writing straight from reading, with no grouping step. *Fix*: organise
by claim; each paragraph makes one point and cites every source that bears on it.

**Topics presented as themes.** *Symptom*: headings such as "Barriers", "Cost",
"Communication". *Cause*: stopping at descriptive grouping. *Fix*: write each as a
sentence that could be wrong.

**Truisms.** *Symptom*: findings nobody would dispute ("implementation quality matters").
*Cause*: abstracting until nothing specific is left. *Fix*: ask what the evidence says
that was not already assumed; state conditions, sizes and exceptions.

**Inventory instead of answer.** *Symptom*: long, complete, and the reader still has to
work out the answer. *Cause*: reporting everything found. *Fix*: lead with the bottom line; move
detail to tables and an appendix.

**Polish without consequence.** *Symptom*: a persuasive narrative and memorable
quotations, but nobody can say which decision it changes. *Cause*: writing to impress. *Fix*: tie each
claim to the decision in the protocol; cut claims that bear on nothing.

**False balance.** *Symptom*: "some studies say X, others say Y" where the two bodies of
evidence are unequal. *Cause*: reluctance to judge. *Fix*: weigh by quality, directness
and number of lineages, and say which side is stronger and why.

**Unexplained contradiction.** *Symptom*: conflicting findings listed side by side.
*Cause*: no attempt to find the reason. *Fix*: work through the moderator checks in
synthesis-methods.md and fill in `reconciled`; if nothing explains it, write `unexplained`.

## Failures of evidence handling

**Invented or reconstructed reference.** *Symptom*: a citation that cannot be found, or
whose details are slightly wrong. *Cause*: writing from memory. *Fix*: cite only from the
ledger; verify each reference resolves and matches.

**Citation that does not support the claim.** *Symptom*: the source exists but says
something weaker, narrower or different. *Cause*: recalling the gist, or relying on an
abstract. *Fix*: the worksheet: re-read at the locator and mark the item.

**Citation laundering.** *Symptom*: a review or article is cited for a finding that
belongs to a study it mentions. *Cause*: not following the chain. *Fix*: go to the
primary source; otherwise write "as reported by" and mark `secondary-citation`.

**Abstract-only reading.** *Symptom*: confident claims from sources never read in full.
*Cause*: paywalls and haste. *Fix*: record the access level; cap certainty; state the
number of sources not read in full.

**Double counting.** *Symptom*: "five sources agree" when they derive from one study or
dataset. *Cause*: counting citations. *Fix*: lineages.

**Vote counting.** *Symptom*: "most studies found a significant effect". *Cause*:
treating significance as a tally. *Fix*: report direction, size and range of effects.

**Cherry-picking.** *Symptom*: all cited evidence points one way. *Cause*: searching for
confirmation; stopping when the expected answer appears. *Fix*: search for the opposite
result, null findings and grey literature; record what was found under `contradicts`.

**Source prestige as proof.** *Symptom*: "published in a leading journal" used as the
reason to believe it. *Cause*: skipping appraisal. *Fix*: appraise the method.

**Stale evidence.** *Symptom*: a superseded finding, retracted paper or outdated figure.
*Cause*: no currency check. *Fix*: check dates, corrections and later work for anything a
main claim depends on.

## Failures of calibration

**Uniform hedging.** *Symptom*: every sentence carries "may", "might", "suggests".
*Cause*: caution applied as a style. *Fix*: rate each claim and use the wording for its
rating.

**Uniform confidence.** *Symptom*: everything stated flatly. *Cause*: no rating step.
*Fix*: as above.

**Single-source certainty.** *Symptom*: a strong claim on one study. *Cause*: a
persuasive source. *Fix*: one lineage allows moderate at most; say that it is unreplicated.

**Overgeneralisation.** *Symptom*: findings from one population, setting or period stated
as general. *Cause*: dropping the context during extraction. *Fix*: keep `context` and
`conditions`; state for whom and where the claim holds.

**Causal language on correlational evidence.** *Symptom*: "leads to", "improves",
"causes" from observational designs. *Cause*: natural phrasing. *Fix*: "is associated
with", unless the design supports cause.

**Absence of evidence as evidence of absence.** *Symptom*: "there is no evidence that X
works" when X has barely been studied. *Cause*: ambiguous phrasing. *Fix*: distinguish
"studies found no effect" from "no adequate studies were found".

**Effect sizes without context.** *Symptom*: interventions ranked by standardised effect
across different kinds of study. *Cause*: treating effect size as a common currency.
*Fix*: see quantitative-evidence.md.

**Anonymous authority.** *Symptom*: "research shows", "experts agree", "it is well
established". *Cause*: filling a gap. *Fix*: cite, or delete.

## Failures of process

**Criteria shifting.** *Symptom*: inclusion rules that happen to admit the congenial
sources. *Cause*: criteria set or changed after reading. *Fix*: fix criteria first; log
any change with its reason.

**Scope creep.** *Symptom*: the synthesis answers a wider or different question than was
asked. *Cause*: following interesting material. *Fix*: return to the question; park the
rest under "related questions".

**Mislabelled review type.** *Symptom*: "systematic review" for a quick search. *Cause*:
borrowed prestige. *Fix*: name the type honestly and state the limits.

**Unreproducible search.** *Symptom*: no record of where or how sources were found.
*Cause*: not logging at the time. *Fix*: search log.

**Premature closure.** *Symptom*: a satisfying story formed early and later evidence was
fitted to it. *Cause*: the pull of a coherent narrative. *Fix*: the Break step (keystones, rival
account, reversal), ideally by a fresh reader.

**Thin data stretched.** *Symptom*: firm themes from three interviews. *Cause*: pressure
to conclude. *Fix*: say what the data can and cannot support and what more is needed.

## Failures of reporting

**Buried answer.** *Symptom*: the conclusion appears on the last page. *Fix*: bottom line
first.

**Finding, interpretation and recommendation blurred.** *Symptom*: the reader cannot tell
what the sources said from what the author thinks. *Fix*: label and separate them.

**Missing method.** *Symptom*: no account of how sources were found, chosen and weighed.
*Fix*: a method section, however short.

**No limitations.** *Symptom*: nothing about what the synthesis could not establish.
*Fix*: state gaps in the evidence and limits of the synthesis separately.

**Quote wall.** *Symptom*: quotations standing in for analysis. *Fix*: one or two per
theme, after the claim they illustrate.

**Unstated assumptions.** *Symptom*: scope decisions the user never saw. *Fix*: list them
at the top.
